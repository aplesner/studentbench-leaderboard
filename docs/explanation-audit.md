# Development explanation audit

The saved 100-pair GRE Quant run contains 1,100 model calls and 8,800 criterion explanations across 81 pretest subjects. These previously inspected subjects belong to development data. The audit used saved model responses and input payloads only; it did not load human verdicts from additional subjects.

The local reproducible audit is `results/ranking-study-20260910/audit/audit.py`, with machine-readable results, the deterministic sample, and manual annotations beside it. The separate `provenance_check.py` traces answer-key edits. Raw examples stay in ignored local results.

## Findings

- **Direction encoding fails in the explanations themselves.** A deterministic sample of 48 explanations, six per judge/member/order stratum, contains 15 clear contradictions between the textual preference and raw signed score. All 15 occur among the 36 GPT explanations in that sample; the Claude and Gemini strata contain only six examples each. An additional targeted sample contains 15 confirmed contradictions and one false-positive screen among 16 candidates. This is a small, equally allocated stratum sample reviewed by one analyst, not a population error estimate. Automatic explicit-winner patterns flag 100 of 8,800 outputs and miss many narrative contradictions; never auto-flip scores from these patterns.
- **Order effects dominate same-order variation.** AB/BA changed 621/800 directions, including 514 opposite nonzero preferences. Independent same-order repeats changed 155/800, including 69 opposite nonzero preferences. All 1,100 raw response parses and swapped payloads, plus 8,800 sign normalizations, passed consistency checks. These checks locate the observed problem inside the saved responses rather than the numeric swap adapter.
- **Five random orders do not guarantee balanced exposure.** Four `judge_3` pairs received five BA presentations. Across 100 pairs, the AB-call counts were 0:4, 1:14, 2:34, 3:30, 4:18. Random majority exposure can translate slot bias into an aggregate preference.
- **Accuracy explanations can invent errors or miss malformed questions.** A manually recomputed weighted mean confirms that one Claude explanation incorrectly criticized a valid answer. A Gemini explanation declaring every answer accurate missed a single-choice geometry question with two valid answers. These examples support independent item checks and explicit verification coverage; they do not estimate overall mathematical error rates.
- **The pretest export lacks some evidence.** Thirteen of 16 unique quantitative-comparison questions in the sample lack explicit compared quantities, and six chart-dependent items lack the shared chart. The upstream review template supports rendering quantities, shared data, and figures. The CSV-based judge payload therefore may have less evidence than the original reviewer interface. Do not reconstruct missing content from plan claims.
- **Stored plans include upstream answer edits.** Seventeen of 2,388 sampled practice problems carry an `answer_original` field different from the displayed `answer`. Upstream `studentbench/answer_verify.py` applies this change before live and review plans are persisted; the review template displays the modified answer. Its equation parser can truncate a factorial or product definition and solve for the defined number instead of the requested quantity. This can turn an originally correct trailing-zero answer into an incorrect key. The local parser reproducer documents the concrete cases. This is an upstream postprocessing issue, not the leaderboard adapter. Current source plus saved artifacts corroborate the mechanism; a historical deployment replay was not performed.
- **Some arguments reward proxies.** The sample includes preferences based on more practice problems, explicit question-ID references, broader coverage, very small time differences, and harder exercises after hard questions were missed. These may or may not improve learning. Grouping, relevance, and difficulty rationales sometimes overlap, risking repeated reward for the same feature across criteria.
- **Embedded author labels are not the obvious cause.** No literal plan A/B or GPT/Claude/Gemini name matches were found inside the 181 unique sampled plan bodies. This is a narrow string check, not proof that stylistic author identification is impossible.

## Judge designs to evaluate

1. **Evidence then winner.** Require one observation per plan, a criterion-specific tradeoff, `winner` in A/B/tie/insufficient, and a separate strength. Convert to signed preferences in code. Remove the irrelevant 1–5 quality labels from this pairwise prompt. Check that missing evidence stays distinct from equal quality.
2. **Independent quality ratings.** Rate each plan against anchored criterion standards before deriving a comparison. Evaluate both orderings and compare the resulting model leaderboard against direct winners. A five-point quality scale can have ceiling effects, so retain the continuous rating difference as a sensitivity analysis.
3. **Balanced order averages.** Use exactly one AB and one BA call, or two of each; normalize and average win probabilities rather than requiring unanimity. Carry disagreement forward as uncertainty instead of discarding most comparisons. Compare with a single randomized presentation at the same total call budget.
4. **Independent correctness audit.** Check every practice item against its stem, choices, and claimed solution; record verified, defective, and unresolved items and require a concrete counterexample for an error claim. Use a stronger reasoning model or a constrained symbolic check for this criterion. Avoid unconstrained auto-repair of source plans.
5. **Diverse evidence panel.** Combine independent judges after evidence-led individual evaluations. Use balanced exposure per member and assess marginal ranking improvement per call. A panel can repeat the same shared prompt or data error, so diversity alone is not a fix.

Keep source-model identity out of all judge prompts. Judge model and effort changes are subversions of a fixed strategy. New aggregation or decomposition strategies receive new judge IDs. Select on validation model-ranking alignment and uncertainty, then evaluate the frozen choice once on held-out subjects. Per-pair human agreement remains a diagnostic rather than the optimization target.

The primary leaderboard estimates quality of the stored generation pipeline, including upstream postprocessing. A sensitivity excluding the answer-accuracy criterion or affected plan pairs can reveal whether answer edits dominate ranks. Restoring `answer_original` without new human labels changes the evaluated object and must not be presented as a human-alignment improvement.
